Features

The four stages of maintenance — intake, dispatch, execution, control — in one service. Below is what already works in the product.

  • Voice work orders
  • Photo and camera
  • Telegram
  • Proof-based payment
  • Roles
  • Offline
  • Preventive maintenance
  • Floor plans
  • Documents
  • Reports
  • Regions and access zones

Voice work orders

Tap “Hey Helpy” and speak. The text appears as you talk; speech recognition is done by the browser (Chrome, Edge) or by Android.

  • AI (YandexGPT) turns the phrase into work order fields: title, description, system (HVAC, electrical, plumbing…), room and priority.
  • If the AI does not answer in time, a built-in dictionary parses the request; the screen shows which one did: “Parsed by AI” or “Parsed by dictionary”.
  • Before sending, a person checks and can edit any field. Typing is always an option.
  • The contractor is assigned automatically by system and site.

Photo and the camera assistant

A technician or employee points the phone camera at a device and taps “What is this?”.

  • A multimodal model describes the shot: equipment type (from a catalogue of 22 types), system, readable nameplate, visible defect.
  • Which exact unit from the register it is, the service decides itself — by serial number, model, type, room and floor. Up to three candidates with reasons.
  • Device card: open work orders, next preventive maintenance, last completed job.
  • “Create work order” — site, room, equipment and system are pre-filled, the shot is attached as the “before” photo.
  • If there is no equipment in the frame, the service says so. The recognition shot is neither stored nor logged.

Work orders and notifications in Telegram

  • Guests via QR code. An A4 poster with a QR code hangs at the site. Anyone, without an account, messages the bot with text, a voice note or a photo — a work order is created for that site. Guests see only the site of their link; the number of requests is limited.
  • Team groups. A Telegram group is linked to a site with a code from the app; the bot reads only messages addressed to it.
  • Employees link their account with a code and create work orders with the same permissions as in the app.
  • The requester receives “in progress” and “done ✅” — with the “after” photo. Notifications can be turned off.
  • The technician receives each new work order with a “Take it” button.

Proof-based payment

The monthly contractor act is built automatically and splits work orders into three groups:

  1. Payable — accepted by a manager, with an “after” photo (if required), a visit inside the site’s geofence (if the site has coordinates) and a fully completed checklist.
  2. Needs a manager’s decision — for example, the time of an offline photo is not verified, the photo was uploaded from a file rather than taken with the camera, or it arrived after the work order was cancelled. The manager approves it for payment (a comment is required) or rejects it — the decision is recorded.
  3. No proof — with the reason: no “after” photo, visit outside the geofence, no visit, work not accepted.

Amounts are calculated from the contractor’s rates “per work order” and “per maintenance visit”. The act gets a number like АКТ-2026-001 and exports to PDF and Excel; a re-issued act gets a new number and the previous one is marked “replaced”.

What counts as proof

  • “After” photo — live app camera only, no gallery. A stamp is burnt into the image: date and time, site, room, “inside geofence”. A photo from a file (possible only in a browser without a camera) is labelled and does not count as proof in the act.
  • Geofenced visit — the time, distance to the site and whether it is inside the radius (150 m by default) are computed by the database, not the phone.
  • Checklist — who ticked each item and when is recorded by the database.

Roles and segregation of duties

Who can do what with a work order is enforced by the database (access policies and triggers), not just the interface. Going around the app does not get around the rules.

Who does what with a work order
ActionWho
Create a work orderEmployee (requester), manager, Telegram guest via the site link
Assign contractor, due date, priorityManager with rights to the site and system, administrator
Start work, check in, tick the checklist, take the “after” photo, submit for reviewOnly a technician of the assigned contractor
Accept or return the workManager or administrator — but not the person who submitted it
CancelManager; the author — until work has started

A manager cannot close work on a contractor’s behalf. If a company has its own maintenance team, it is set up as a contractor, and another manager or the administrator accepts the work.

Working offline

Basements, roofs and plant rooms often have no signal. In the Android app the technician keeps working without it:

  • their work orders with checklists, rooms, floor plans and “before” photos are saved on the phone in advance;
  • start, check in, tick the checklist, take the “after” photo, submit — actions are queued and sent automatically once the connection is back;
  • trusted time: the time of each action is counted from the last server contact using a clock that cannot be changed, or from GPS; if the phone clock is off by more than 5 minutes, the time is marked “not verified”;
  • photo fingerprint (SHA-256) is taken on the phone and verified on the server — a swapped file is detected;
  • disputed cases (time not verified, work order cancelled while offline) land in the act under “Needs a manager’s decision”.

Signing out deletes everything stored on the phone. The web version does not work offline.

Preventive maintenance

  • A maintenance plan: system, site or a specific piece of equipment, frequency — month, quarter, half-year, year or every N days, checklist.
  • Tasks for each period are created automatically, without duplicates, and go to the assigned contractor.
  • See how much is done this month, what has not started and what is overdue; each plan keeps 12 periods of history.
  • Maintenance visits are paid at their own rate in the contractor act.

Floor plans and equipment

  • Floor plans are uploaded as images (PNG, JPEG, WebP); rooms and equipment are placed on the plan as points.
  • Markers are coloured by the most urgent work order, equipment shows the number of open work orders; “Show on plan” from a work order.
  • Asset register: type, system, manufacturer, model, serial number, installation date, work order history and maintenance plans. Import from Excel and CSV using a template.
  • Sites on a map, clustered by city; grouped region → country → city.

Documents

Documents can be attached to a work order: PDF, DOC(X), XLS(X), JPG, PNG, TXT — up to 20 MB. Files are private: they open via a temporary link and only for those who can see the work order.

Reports

  • Per contractor for a period: geofenced visits, time on site, share of work done on time, first-time acceptance, returns.
  • Breakdowns by region and work order type, photo recognition statistics.
  • Report as PDF in one tap; contractor act as PDF and Excel.
  • History of accepted and cancelled work orders for a period.

Regions and access zones

  • A portfolio of sites across cities and countries: regions, countries, cities.
  • A manager’s access zone is “systems × places”, e.g. “HVAC · Moscow”: the manager sees and changes only their part.
  • Contractor crews: a technician sees only the work orders in their crew’s zone.
  • Access change log (roles, zones, crews) — administrator only, read-only.
  • Interface in Russian and English.
